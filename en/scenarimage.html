Water Loss at Le Mérisier

Production storyboard for the interactive module “Water Loss at Le Mérisier.” Template and content by Thierry De Gagné. The case, people, documents and amounts are fictitious.

Project leadThierry De Gagné
Instructional designThierry De Gagné
Subject matter expertFictitious — claims and co-ownership practice
DevelopmentHTML and JavaScript, built through vibe coding
Target audienceClaims adjusters, property damage
Target duration12 to 18 minutes, depending on the number of steps taken
DeliveryStandalone web page, no plug-in
LanguagesFrench and English

General guidelines

Menu
No free menu. Progress through locked steps: scenario, investigation, decision, feedback. Learners decide when to move to the next step, but cannot go back.
Structure
Four main screens, a document reader in an overlay window, and a debrief assembled from the learner's actual path.
Characters
Six contacts, reachable by phone or by email. Each has a distinct voice: the co-owner is exasperated, the property manager is efficient and a little curt, the chair thinks budget, the neighbour just wants repairs, the contractor talks job site, the broker is cautious.
Glossary
Common portions · standard unit · betterment · deductible · syndicate · fraction. Terms are never defined out of context: learners discover them in the documents on file.
Variables
franchise (deductible) drawn at random from $10,000, $15,000, $20,000 and $25,000 on each attempt · demarches action counter · documents list of documents obtained · repartition sorting answers.
Design rule
No feedback reveals information the learner did not obtain through their own steps. Missing information is named as such, and each error is linked to the step that was skipped.

Screens

Screen 01Scenariostatic
Visual
File header: number 26-8871, syndicate name, unit concerned. No illustration: the page looks like a claim file.
On-screen text

“You are the claims adjuster on this file.” Description of the loss: a fitting failed on a supply riser inside the party wall of 302; hardwood floor swelling, lower cabinets soaked, ceiling of 202 stained over two metres.

Objectives: choose whom to contact; tell common portions, the standard unit and betterments apart; allocate the damage; determine whether the syndicate's share exceeds the deductible.

Narration

NAR_01.mp3

“It's 8:15. Your first call of the day is from a co-owner who wants to know who pays for her floor. You have nothing on file yet.”

Feedback
None.
Actions and branching
“Open the file” button → screen 02. Permanent note at the bottom of the screen: fictitious case. The franchise variable is drawn on load.
Screen 02Investigationinteractive · core of the module
Visual
Two areas. On the left, the six contacts, each with two buttons: Call, Email. On the right, the file thread as it grows, and the list of documents received.
On-screen text

Counter: “Steps taken: N · documents on file: N.” Instruction: a call gets an answer right away; an email brings back documents, but the reply arrives the next day.

No limit on steps. The instruction states that they will be compared, at the end, with the minimum required.

Narration

NAR_02.mp3

“It's up to you to decide whom you contact, and how. Every call gets an immediate answer; every email brings back documents, but costs you a day.”

Feedback
No evaluative feedback at this step. Each step produces the contact's reply, and a note reading “Added to the file” or “Nothing new for the file.”
Actions and branching
  • Property manager — email: declaration of co-ownership + syndicate's policy. Phone: gives the cause and refers to the chair for the schedule.
  • Chair — email: schedule describing the standard unit. Phone: talks deductible and premiums, without giving the amount.
  • Contractor — email: site report + repair estimate + plumber's invoice. Phone: on-site verdict, no figures.
  • Co-owner — email: her policy, with the betterments endorsement. Phone: her version of events.
  • Broker — email: copy of the syndicate's policy (alternate route). Phone: restates the principle, refuses to quote the deductible from memory.
  • Neighbour in 202 — phone only: confirms the ceiling is original.

Each step can be taken only once. “Move on to the decision” button active at all times → screen 03. Minimum path: three emails (property manager, chair, contractor).

Screen 02bReading a documentoverlay window
Visual
Overlay window, document title, scrolling body, Close button. Laid out like an official excerpt: section headings in small capitals, running text, a table for the estimate.
On-screen text
  • Declaration of co-ownership: section 41, common portions, including risers that serve more than one fraction; section 42, which refers to a separate schedule; section 43, betterments remain the co-owner's responsibility.
  • Description of the standard unit: schedule C adopted at the general meeting of June 12, 2019, six items listed, including nylon carpet or vinyl flooring and white melamine cabinets.
  • Syndicate's policy: insured property, exclusion of betterments, deductible, amount varying with each attempt.
  • Co-owner's policy: betterments endorsement $30,000, contents $60,000, deductible $1,000.
  • Site report: cause, water category, inventory of damage by unit.
  • Repair estimate: six items, total $34,600, with no allocation.
  • Plumber's invoice: emergency call, $1,340, original fitting.
Narration
None. Reading is silent, as it is on a real file.
Feedback
None.
Actions and branching
Documents remain available on screen 03, during the decision. The policy text is generated with the franchise value drawn at the start of the attempt.
Screen 03Allocation and deductibledrag-and-drop + multiple choice
Visual
Five labels to sort into two columns: syndicate's insurance, co-owner's insurance. The document list stays visible on the right. Below the sort, two multiple-choice questions.
On-screen text

Items to allocate: the fitting and the supply riser; the melamine cabinets in 302; the drywall ceiling in 202; the hardwood floor in 302; the living-room furniture.

Question 1: what is the deductible on the syndicate's policy? Four choices, from $10,000 to $25,000. Question 2: does the syndicate's insured share exceed that deductible? Two choices.

Narration

NAR_03.mp3

“Now allocate the damage, then tell me whether it's worth the syndicate's while to file a claim.”

Feedback
None at this step: validation is delayed to prevent trial-and-error answers.
Actions and branching
Drag and drop, or select the label and then the column, for touch and keyboard users. “Submit my answers” button inactive until all five labels are placed and both questions answered → screen 04.
Screen 04Feedback and debriefassembled from the learner's path
Visual
A line of results in figures, then one block per item, green or red, then the debrief notes in boxes.
On-screen text

Results: items correctly placed out of five · deductible correct or not · exceeding correct or not · number of steps against a required minimum of three.

Narration

NAR_04.mp3

“Let's look at your file.”

Positive feedback
  • Riser: “The riser serves several fractions: it is a common portion (section 41).”
  • Cabinets: “The schedule places white melamine cabinets in the standard unit.”
  • Ceiling in 202: “Original painted drywall. The fact that the water came from another unit changes nothing.”
  • Floor: “The schedule provides for carpet or vinyl: hardwood is a betterment (section 43).”
  • Furniture: “Contents were never part of the building.”
Negative feedback
Same explanation, preceded by the answer given and the correct answer. No wording that judges the person.
Actions and branching

Debrief notes displayed according to conditions, not all at once:

  • schedule missing from the file → “you decided without the only document that says whether the hardwood is a betterment”;
  • declaration missing → nothing establishes that the riser is a common portion;
  • syndicate's policy missing → the deductible could only be guessed;
  • estimate missing → the comparison with the deductible rests on no figures;
  • report missing → the cause is not established in writing;
  • three steps or fewer and no errors → optimal path named explicitly;
  • more than six steps → the real cost of unnecessary steps for the insured.

Note always displayed: the deductible remains the syndicate's responsibility and is not billed to the co-owner, even when her betterments are involved. “Start over” button: reset and new deductible draw.

Expected answers

Syndicate's insurance
Fitting and riser · melamine cabinets in 302 · drywall ceiling in 202. Corresponding share: $19,500, i.e. plumbing and wall $4,200, cabinets $6,800, ceiling $3,100, drying $5,400.
Co-owner's insurance
Hardwood floor $11,200 · furniture $3,900. Total of $15,100, excluded from the syndicate's deductible calculation.
Deductible
Value drawn at random. With a $19,500 share: a claim is justified at $10,000 and $15,000; a claim is pointless at $20,000 and $25,000.
The central trap of the scenario: the declaration of co-ownership is not enough. It refers to a schedule adopted at a general meeting, held only by the chair. A learner in a hurry concludes that the floor is covered by the syndicate, because nothing in the declaration says otherwise.
Storyboard written by Thierry De Gagné, September 2026, for his portfolio. The template follows the production storyboards he used in corporate settings: one block per screen, on-screen text, narration with audio file names, separate feedback, and a column of actions and branching for the development team.
